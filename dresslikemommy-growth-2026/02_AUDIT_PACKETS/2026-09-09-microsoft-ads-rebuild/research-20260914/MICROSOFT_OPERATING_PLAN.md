**4. Account blueprint**

The account has no meaningful paid-learning baseline. Historical store orders remain useful for product economics and language demand, but mixed-source site conversion rates are not Microsoft forecasts.

**Economics before budgets.** Define R as merchandise revenue after discounts and refunds, excluding tax and shipping charges. Define S as retained shipping revenue. Define K as disjoint actual product cost, outbound fulfillment/freight, payment fees, return handling and other variable costs, net of evidenced recoveries. Pre-ad contribution C = R + S − K.

For a first-order cohort, break-even acquisition allowance is C; desired allowance is C − required profit reserve P. Revenue-based break-even ROAS is R/C; target ROAS is R/(C−P), only when the denominator is positive. These are contribution measures, not company net profit after fixed overhead. CAC uses distinct new customers; order CPA uses distinct purchases. They are interchangeable only in an explicitly first-order-only cohort.

The [September 11 economic contract](/Users/fsuels/Projects/dresslikemommy/dresslikemommy-growth-2026/02_AUDIT_PACKETS/2026-09-09-microsoft-ads-rebuild/launch-plan-20260911/economics.json) retained conservative planning limits of 15% total marketing, a 6.67 revenue/spend floor and 30% of R reserved as contribution after marketing. Preserve these pending an owner decision; they are not invoice-backed margins or a spending authorization. Under those assumptions, total allowable marketing A = min(0.15R, R/6.67, C−0.30R). Microsoft can use only the remaining allocation after other marketing commitments.

Separately include nonrefundable fees, return labels, processing and unrecovered product cost; record supplier credits only when real. Keep estimated return reserves and settled actual returns in separate views and replace the estimate when matured. Do not use unproved lifetime value to rescue an unprofitable first order.

The launch budget remains **unset**. The owner must define a 30-day cash budget B and maximum test loss L, considering all other marketing. Since zero sales are possible, initial spend at risk cannot exceed the accepted loss. Allocate one cohort's test envelope first; do not treat saved $5/day U.S. and $3/day Germany settings as approved amounts. A daily budget is not itself proof of a hard total-loss stop; verify native pacing and retain headroom for reporting/stop delays.

**7. Phased 90-day launch and management plan**

These are planning windows from an approved launch, not deadlines that force a decision with inadequate data.

| Window | Work and concrete decision |
|---|---|
| Prelaunch | Close the event/consent contract, actual-cost table, selected-product/country QA, native controls, exact budget/loss decision and independent review. Produce GO or one prioritized failed gate. |
| First 72 hours | Check spending, billed CPC distribution, real locations, network/publishers, destinations and sender health on each scheduled run. Reconcile legitimate orders after receiver/reporting delays. Fix clear technical waste; avoid daily bid experiments. |
| Days 4–30 | Review mature query/cohort evidence, exclude clearly irrelevant intent, inspect product exits/size availability, compare actual first-order contribution with allowance. Diagnose nonserving promptly; do not confuse paused status with auction failure. |
| Days 31–60 | Refine one constrained factor at a time. Evaluate selected-product Shopping or Germany only when their independent prerequisites and sample budget pass. Evaluate purchase bidding from trustworthy signals and exact current eligibility, not an arbitrary elapsed day. |
| Days 61–90 | Scale only repeated positive contribution with return maturity and enough loss headroom. Consider one additional market, PMax or remarketing experiment; keep non-overlapping product/traffic reporting where practical. Extend learning if data remains thin. |

**Daily:** review cash at risk, tracking/consent anomalies, disapprovals, relevant size availability and obviously irrelevant traffic. Decision: continue safely, execute an authorized bounded correction, or hold the affected cohort.

**Weekly:** reconcile distinct orders/new customers, country/product contribution, search terms, placement quality, conversion delay and return reserves. Decide the one next experiment and budget reallocation; retain a dated before/after record.

**Monthly:** reconcile settled costs/refunds and all marketing commitments, compare mature cohorts, review access/automation changes and assess incrementality. Decide whether growth increased contribution rather than merely attributed revenue.

**Stop-loss design.** Agree total L and a cohort allocation before spending. Immediate operational problems—broken checkout, wrong geography/value/currency, duplicates, compromised consent or unavailable advertised variants—are technical stops, not statistical experiments. Stop or reduce only under the exact established authority; this report grants none.

For commercial underperformance, compare mature cumulative contribution minus spend with the cohort loss allowance. When attribution is too uncertain, do not credit unverifiable revenue to enlarge the allowance. Keep spend headroom for lag and the time required to stop delivery. A lack of conversions before the observed conversion delay has elapsed is not enough by itself to declare failure.

A useful **illustrative statistical screen**, not a universal stop rule: with an assumed 1.5% purchase rate and independent comparable clicks, zero purchases after 199 fully matured clicks has roughly a 5% probability. At exactly $0.15/click those clicks cost $29.85. This does not establish actual demand, click quality or the affordable budget; repeated peeking, mixed cohorts and delayed orders weaken the calculation. Use uncertainty intervals, not a fixed “three days” rule.

If an eligible approved campaign has zero impressions after 24 hours, diagnose auction eligibility, demand, negative conflicts, location/language, bid cap and scheduling that day. Prepare a small high-intent long-tail alternative. Do not automatically broaden audiences or raise the cap.

**Scaling criterion:** repeated mature cohorts meet the actual contribution target, receiver reconciliation remains trustworthy, supplier capacity is credible and the next spend increment fits the owner envelope. Change one factor, freeze a comparable observation window, then re-evaluate marginal profit. A 10–15% increment can be a provisional operational heuristic if affordable; it is not a Microsoft rule or a guarantee that learning is preserved. Roll back when marginal contribution fails the agreed condition.

For incrementality, first separate own-brand and repeat-customer demand. When volume supports it, use a comparable geographic holdout or time-based test with seasonality/other-channel confounders recorded. With tiny samples, report incrementality as unknown rather than calling platform attribution causal lift.

**9. Do not do this**

- Launch 21 language campaigns because translations exist, or select countries because a generic CPC benchmark is cheap.
- Say tracking is perfect because UET is Healthy, an event name appeared or one purchase arrived.
- Add app, theme, custom pixel and CAPI purchase senders without a single-owner dedup design.
- Hardcode consent, inject fake purchases/click identifiers, or rebuild deprecated checkout scripts.
- Treat add-to-cart, ad strength, platform recommendations or AI-assisted session uplift as profit.
- Assume eCPC, all automated bids or portfolio migration preserve a hard per-click ceiling.
- Enable PMax/all products, broad matching, automatic copy or scheduled imports before their controls are reviewed.
- Use invented supplier facts, materials, measurements, reviews, scarcity, discounts or local inventory.
- Advertise a mother-and-child pair at the child's single-piece price.
- Count refunded revenue, tax, estimated future repeat purchases or another channel's sales as proven acquisition profit.
- Raise spending because gross revenue rose while contribution per order fell.
- Reaudit an unchanged blocker indefinitely; advance another useful preparation lane and keep one explicit decision ready.
