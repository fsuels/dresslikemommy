The easiest way to match on a family cruise is to pick one color palette for the whole trip, then choose three or four matching looks: one for port days, one for the dinner or formal night, and one for the pool deck. You do not need to match every day. Two or three coordinated moments, planned around the photos you actually want, look better than seven days of identical outfits. This guide walks through the plan, the pieces that work for each part of the trip, how to size a family that spans ages, and how to pack it all in one suitcase each.

Start With One Palette, Not Ten Outfits

Before you shop, choose two or three colors that will carry the whole trip. Navy, white, and one accent such as coral, sage, or sunny yellow is a reliable base because it looks good against ocean, sky, and ship interiors alike. Everything you pack should belong to that palette, so pieces mix freely: a matching shirt on port day can pair with solid shorts you also wear on the pool deck.

A palette also makes the trip easier for people who dislike matching. Dad can wear a solid navy shirt, a teenager can wear the accent color, and the family still reads as coordinated in every photo. Save the fully identical outfits for the one or two moments that matter most.

Sizing a Family Across Ages

Size each person on their own, not as a set. Open the size chart on the product page, compare it with a piece that already fits well, and choose from the measurements rather than the age label. Children can grow between ordering and sailing, so if your trip is months away, leave room for that. For a deeper walkthrough of sizing everyone, see our matching outfit sizing guide.

Packing Tips

  • Lay out every outfit on the bed first and cut anything that does not fit the palette.
  • Roll tops instead of folding them to save space and reduce creases.
  • Pack the dinner outfits in your carry-on so the key photos are covered even if a bag is delayed.
  • Bring one spare matching top for a child, since spills happen.
  • Read the care instructions on each product page and pack accordingly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do we need to match every day of the cruise?

No. Two or three matching moments, usually a port day and a dinner night, are plenty. A shared color palette keeps everything else looking coordinated.

What if someone in the family does not like matching?

Let them wear a solid piece in one of the trip colors. In photos the family still looks coordinated, and everyone stays comfortable.

What is the best matching outfit for a cruise dinner?
