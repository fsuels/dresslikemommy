The simplest way to plan maternity Christmas photos with the family is to treat the mom-to-be's dress as the anchor, then pull one or two of its colors into everyone else's clothes. A gown that frames the bump sets the tone, and a shared palette in simple tops, sweaters or pajamas makes the whole group look planned without being identical. This guide covers how to choose the main dress, build a palette, dress kids and partners, and set up a shoot that works for a pregnant mom and a busy family.

Start With the Dress for the Bump

In a family maternity photo the bump is the focus, so the first decision is what the mom-to-be wears. A few shapes tend to photograph well:

  • A flowing gown with a fitted bodice. It shows the bump through the waist or midsection while the skirt adds movement for twirls and wind-blown shots.
  • A stretch fitted dress. Clean lines that follow the bump suit simple studio or living room settings.
  • A sheer or open waist detail. It frames the bump and works well for close-up shots with a partner or older child.

Build a Palette From the Dress

Once the dress is chosen, decide which one or two colors from it will repeat. A few combinations are easy to follow:

  • Sky blue gown with white and cream. Soft winter shades that photograph brightly indoors or in snow.
  • Deep red or burgundy with cream and charcoal. A classic Christmas palette that suits warmer indoor lighting.
  • Forest green with ivory. Rich and festive without being tied to one tradition.
  • Neutral gown with one accent. Choose a champagne or ivory dress and add a single red or green piece for the rest of the family.

Keep prints small and use no more than three colors across the group. Our family photo color palettes by season guide has more combinations to borrow.

Dress the Partner and Kids Around Her

The rest of the family should support the dress, not compete with it. Simple pieces in the shared palette work best:

  • Partner. A solid sweater, collared shirt or knit in cream, navy or charcoal. If the gown is bright, keep this one quiet.
  • Older kids. Sweaters or tops in the palette. Browse family sweaters for knits that come in child and adult sizes so the group looks coordinated.
  • Toddlers. Comfort wins. Soft fabrics and an easy neckline keep a toddler happy through a longer shoot.

Plan Two Looks: Formal and Cozy

Many families do two outfit changes. The first is the formal photo with the gown and coordinated tops, and the second is a relaxed one in pajamas or sweaters by the tree.

  • Formal look. The gown plus palette-matched tops, for the card portrait and the close-ups of the bump.
  • Cozy look. Matching or coordinating pajamas for a candid morning-in-the-living-room feel. See Christmas pajamas for family sets, and our matching family Christmas pajamas guide for how to choose a print.

Two looks give you a card-ready image and a few relaxed shots, and you will not need to decide between them.

Sizing When You Are Pregnant

Ordering a photo outfit during pregnancy takes a little extra thought:

  • Use the size chart on the product page. Compare your bust, waist and hips and choose by the measurements that matter for the style.
  • Think about the date of the shoot. Your bump will be larger in a few weeks, so size for the day of the photos rather than today. Check that the waist or skirt leaves room.
  • Allow for layers. If the shoot is outdoors in cold weather, leave space for a coat or wrap.
  • Check kids' sizes separately. Compare each child's measurements with the chart instead of guessing from their age.

Shoot-Day Tips

  • Time it for comfort. Many people plan the shoot for the middle of the third trimester or earlier if they want to stay comfortable standing. Choose a date that suits your own pregnancy.
  • Bring a wrap. A neutral cardigan or coat keeps you warm between shots without hiding the dress.
  • Steam the day before. Tulle and chiffon wrinkle in transit, so steam on a low setting and hang it overnight.
  • Keep it short. Plan the poses and groupings in advance so the shoot is quick and kids stay patient.
  • Choose a simple background. A plain wall, a decorated tree or a snowy field keeps the focus on the family.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should a pregnant mom wear for Christmas family photos?

A flowing gown with a fitted bodice or a stretch fitted dress both work well. Choose a color from your family's palette and make sure the waist leaves room for the bump on the day of the shoot.

How do we coordinate everyone's outfits around the bump?

Choose one or two colors from the maternity dress and repeat them in simple tops, sweaters or pajamas for everyone else. Keep prints small and avoid more than three colors.

Do we all need to match?

No. Coordinated colors look intentional and are easier to wear. If you want an identical set for some shots, pick one matching outfit design offered in child and adult sizes.
