The easiest way to match for a cold-weather outing is to put everyone in the same sweater or sweatshirt and let the coats, hats and scarves do the rest. A shared top keeps the family looking like a group at a tree farm, skating rink or hot-cocoa stop, while layers keep each person comfortable. This guide covers how to choose the base layer, plan for different ages, and pick colors that read well outdoors in winter light.

Start With One Shared Base Layer

Outdoor outings are mostly hidden under coats, so the piece that shows when jackets come off matters most. Pick one design for the family and treat it as the base:

  • Crewneck sweaters look neat under an open coat and photograph well. Browse family sweaters to compare designs offered in child and adult sizes.
  • Sweatshirts are softer and more casual, which suits a long day outside. The matching family sweatshirts collection has plenty of options.
  • Long-sleeve tops work best when you expect to be indoors for part of the day. See family tops for lighter layers.

Plan the Outfit Around the Outing

Tree farm or pumpkin-patch style walk

Uneven ground and a lot of walking call for sweatshirts or sweaters with room for a thermal layer underneath. Earthy colors such as forest green, rust and cream hide a little mud and look at home among the trees.

Ice skating

You will move a lot and warm up quickly, so choose a fitted crewneck that sits comfortably under a light jacket. Skip anything with long, loose cuffs that can get in the way.

Hot-cocoa and market day

This is the easiest outing to match. Wear the same sweater, add jeans, and let each person choose a scarf or hat. It photographs well in front of lights and storefronts.

Cozy evenings after the outing

If your day ends at home, matching pajamas are a nice finish. Our family pajamas collection pairs well with a sweater-and-coat daytime look.

Dress Kids for Comfort First

Children decide quickly whether an outing is fun, and cold or scratchy clothing ends it. A few habits help:

  • Layer a thin top under the shared sweater rather than choosing a size up.
  • Keep a spare pair of socks and gloves in the bag.
  • Choose one outer layer each child can take on and off alone.
  • Size each child separately with the size chart on the product page, comparing chest and garment length with something that already fits.

Every product page lists its available sizes, so you can confirm each person's size before ordering.

Choose Colors That Stand Out Outdoors

Winter light is flat and grey, so very pale or very dark outfits can disappear in photos. Some combinations that keep their shape:

  • Cream with a deep accent. A white or cream sweater with burgundy scarves and navy coats looks clean against bare trees.
  • Sage green with oatmeal. Soft, natural and easy to repeat across ages.
  • Red with black. Bold enough to find your group in a crowd, and a safe choice for holiday outings.
  • Light blue with grey. Calm and bright against winter skies.

Choose one family color and let accessories bring in a second one. That keeps the look coordinated even if coats do not match.

A Sample Plan for a Family of Four

To make this concrete, here is one way to plan a Saturday outing for two adults and two children:

  • Base layer: the same cream crewneck sweater in each person's size.
  • Bottoms: dark jeans or warm leggings for everyone, so the sweater is the only statement piece.
  • Outer layer: whatever coats you already own, in navy, black or tan.
  • Accessories: burgundy beanies and scarves for all four, one shared accent color.
  • Backup: a thin thermal top for each child and a spare pair of socks in the bag.

This approach costs one purchase per person, works across ages, and still looks coordinated when coats come off for a photo. If your family is larger, add the same sweater in more sizes rather than mixing designs, which keeps the group looking like a set.

Accessories That Tie It Together

When coats are all different colors, accessories become the link. Matching beanies, scarves or mittens in the sweater's accent color make a mixed set of jackets look planned. For a lighter touch, give each person a different accessory in the same palette.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do the whole family need identical coats?

No. Match the base layer under the coat and use accessories in a shared color. This looks coordinated without everyone buying the same jacket.

Are sweaters or sweatshirts warmer for outdoor outings?

Both work when layered. Sweatshirts feel softer and more casual, while crewneck sweaters look a little neater. Add a thin thermal layer underneath for long days outside.

What colors photograph best in winter?

Cream with a deep accent, sage green, red with black and light blue with grey keep their shape against flat winter light.

How should I size children for layering?

Size each child separately using the size chart on the product page, and layer a thin top underneath instead of ordering much larger.
