A holiday lights night is one of the easiest family outings to dress for, and one of the hardest to photograph. The best outfit is warm enough to stand outside for an hour, in colors that stand out against dark skies and bright bulbs, and comfortable enough for children to walk, climb and wiggle in. Choose cozy layers, keep one shared palette, and plan one quick photo near the brightest display. This guide covers what to wear, how to coordinate the family, and how to get a night-time picture that looks like the evening felt.

Start With Warmth, Then Add the Match

Evening temperatures drop quickly after sunset, and a lights walk usually means a lot of standing and slow walking. Dress for the cold first and coordinate second:

  • A warm top layer. Knit sweaters hold heat well and look good in photos. Browse the family sweaters collection.
  • A cozy sweatshirt. Easy to layer over a thermal shirt and simple for children to wear all evening. See matching family sweatshirts.
  • Outer layers in the same palette. Coats, hats and scarves will cover most of the outfit, so coordinate those colors too.

For more cold-weather ideas, explore the fall and winter collection.

Pick Colors That Glow at Night

Camera flashes and string lights change how colors look after dark. A few palette tips help:

  • Cream and ivory. Light tones catch the glow of the lights and keep faces bright.
  • Deep red and burgundy. Rich shades look warm against white and gold bulbs and rarely look washed out.
  • Forest green and navy. Darker colors give contrast behind lighter pieces and hide small smudges from hot cocoa.
  • Use a small amount of plaid. A buffalo plaid scarf or top adds pattern without competing with the display.

Very dark head-to-toe black can disappear in a night photo, so add at least one lighter piece to each outfit.

Match the Family Without Matching Exactly

When everyone is bundled up, a perfect match is hard to see. Coordinating is easier and more flexible:

  • Choose one main color for everyone's top layer.
  • Let each person add a different accent, such as a plaid scarf, a patterned hat or a bright pair of mittens.
  • Keep the children's layers easy to remove, because indoor stops at the gift shop or snack stand can feel warm.

Tree Farm or Lights Walk: Plan for the Setting

The two outings are similar but not identical:

  • Tree farm. Expect uneven ground, so choose comfortable, closed shoes and pieces that do not drag. Our guide to family matching outfits for cold-weather outings covers tree farms and other outdoor days.
  • Neighborhood or park lights walk. More walking and less standing, so lighter layers can work if you plan to keep moving.
  • Drive-through displays. You may stay in the car for part of the evening, so plan one layer that is easy to remove.

A Simple Plan for the Night-Time Group Photo

Night photos can be tricky, but a short plan helps:

  • Choose one display. Pick the brightest or prettiest one and take the photo there instead of at every stop.
  • Stand a few steps in front of the lights. Standing too close makes the bulbs overpower faces; a little distance lets both show.
  • Turn off the flash if the lights are bright. Many phones capture a more natural glow in night mode with a steady hand or a stable surface.
  • Take a few frames and stop. One posed photo and one laughing candid is plenty before the children lose patience.

Keep Children Comfortable

A cheerful family photo starts with children who are warm and comfortable:

  • Pack a spare pair of gloves or socks in case of wet snow or puddles.
  • Choose tops that do not scratch or ride up under a coat.
  • Bring a snack and a warm drink for the walk.
  • Leave extra time for bathroom stops before the photo.

Sizing Everyone Before You Order

If you are ordering several outfits for one outing, a short checklist keeps it simple:

  • Measure each person and compare with the size chart on the product page.
  • Check the chart for each design, because sizing can differ from one design to the next.
  • Choose the larger size for children who are between sizes, especially if layering underneath.
  • Write each person's size next to their name before adding anything to the cart.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should a family wear to see Christmas lights?

Warm layers in one shared palette, with a lighter color or two so everyone shows up well in night photos.

What colors look best in photos with Christmas lights?

Cream, deep red, forest green and navy tend to look good against bulbs. Avoid wearing only black.

Should we match exactly for a lights night?

No. Coats and hats cover most of an outfit, so one shared color with different accents looks coordinated and is easier to plan.

How do we take a good night photo with kids?

Pick one display, stand a few steps in front of the lights, take a few frames and stop before everyone gets cold.
