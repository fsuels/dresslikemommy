The easiest Christmas morning outfit plan is to put the whole family in matching pajamas for the gift opening, then switch into matching sweaters or a coordinated palette for a second round of photos later in the day. Pajamas are the right first outfit because kids wake up early and nobody wants to get dressed before the presents. This guide gives a simple timeline for the morning, shows how to choose between pajamas and sweaters, and covers sizing and quick photo tips so the pictures never slow down the fun.

A Simple Christmas Morning Timeline

Having a rough order of events keeps the photos easy and the morning relaxed:

  • Wake-up. Everyone is already in matching pajamas, whether they put them on the night before or found them in a Christmas Eve box. If you want the box tradition, see our Christmas Eve box ideas with matching pajamas.
  • Before the first present. Take two or three quick group photos by the tree. Kids are curious but not yet distracted by wrapping paper, so this is the cleanest window for a family shot.
  • Gift opening. Put the camera down, or set up a phone on a shelf with a burst or video mode and let it run. The best pictures are the candid ones.
  • Breakfast. Matching pajamas at the table make an easy second photo moment with cocoa, pancakes or cinnamon rolls.
  • Later in the day. Change into a daytime matching outfit for visiting family or a card photo, if that is on your list.

Pajamas or Sweaters for the Morning?

Both work, and many families choose one for the morning and the other for later:

  • Pajamas for the opening. They are comfortable for sitting on the floor for an hour, and a shared print looks festive in every photo. Browse the Christmas pajamas collection for sets in child and adult sizes, and the wider family pajamas collection if you want more prints to compare.

  • Mix and match. If you only want one set, the matching pajamas are the better choice for the morning because the opening is where most of the pictures happen.

Choosing a Look That Works in the Morning Light

Early light and tree lights are softer than a studio, so simple designs photograph best:

  • Plaid and stripes. Red and black buffalo plaid or a classic stripe reads clearly even in a dim room.
  • One shared print. The same design across every size gives a clean group photo without any styling work.
  • Coordinated palette. If everyone prefers a different print, keep to the same red, green or cream family so the photos still look planned.
  • Avoid very busy backgrounds. A cluttered room full of wrapping paper is part of the charm, but a plain wall or the tree behind the group keeps the focus on the outfits.

Sizing Everyone Before the Day

Christmas morning is not the time to find out a pair of pajamas does not fit, so check sizes early:

  • Use the size chart on each product page. Child, women's and men's sizes are listed separately, so measure each person instead of guessing from age.
  • Compare to something that fits now. A favorite pair of pajamas laid flat is an easy reference for chest, waist and length.
  • Write one list. Put every family member's size together before ordering so nobody is left out.
  • Mixing ages. Our matching family pajamas sizing guide explains how to order one print across kids and adults.

Photo Tips for a Busy Morning

  • Do the group shot first. Two minutes by the tree before the first present gets you the picture you want without anyone fidgeting.
  • Use a timer or tripod. The parent who usually takes the photos can be in them too.
  • Shoot candids. Let the kids open presents naturally and take a few pictures from across the room.
  • Keep it short. A handful of frames is enough. Delete the extras later rather than posing everyone for ten minutes.
  • Save the formal photos. If you want a card or a dressier portrait, plan it for a separate time. Our Christmas photo outfits guide and card photo ideas cover the options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should the family wear on Christmas morning?

Matching pajamas are the easiest choice for the gift opening because they are comfortable and photograph well. Many families change into matching sweaters or a coordinated palette later for visits and dinner.

When should we take the family photo?

Take two or three quick shots by the tree before the first present is opened. Kids are curious but not yet distracted, so it is the cleanest window for a group picture.

Do the outfits have to match exactly?

No. One shared print gives the strongest photo, but two prints in the same color palette also look coordinated and let people choose a pair they like.

How do I size pajamas for the whole family?

Use the size chart on each product page and measure each person separately. Child, women's and men's sizes are listed on their own, so compare chest, waist and length instead of guessing by age.
