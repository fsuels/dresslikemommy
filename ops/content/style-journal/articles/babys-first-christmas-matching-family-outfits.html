The easiest way to dress the family for baby's first Christmas is to choose one shared color palette, then let each person wear the piece that suits their day. Baby gets something soft and easy to change, and everyone else picks up the same colors or the same print. This guide covers what works for a baby, how to check that a design comes in a small enough size, how to size for a baby who is growing fast, and a few photo tips that make the most of a first Christmas.

Start With Baby, Then Build the Family Look Around Them

A baby's outfit has to do two jobs: look good in photos and stay comfortable through naps, feedings, and diaper changes. Because of that, plan around baby first and let the rest of the family follow. Pick the piece for the baby, note its main color, and choose adult and sibling outfits that repeat it.

Not every matching design is made in a baby size, so check before you fall in love with one. Many matching designs start at toddler sizes, and only some include a 6-12 months size. Three Ways to Match With a Baby

1. Same design in every size

If the design you love includes a baby size, this is the simplest route. Everyone wears the same print, and the photo reads as a family at a glance. Confirm the smallest size on the product page, then choose sizes for each person separately.

2. Same palette, different pieces

Baby wears a soft cream or red piece while parents wear sweaters in a related shade. Color does the matching, so it does not matter if the design is not offered in every size. This is the most flexible option and works well when the baby's size is hard to find in a matching print.

3. Pajamas for the morning, a sweater for the photo

Christmas morning usually happens in pajamas, so consider matching there. Browse the Christmas pajamas and the wider family pajamas collection to compare designs and size ranges. Then keep a sweater or shirt for the formal photo. Two simple looks often feel easier than one outfit worn all day.

Palettes That Photograph Well With a Baby

  • Burgundy and cream. Warm and calm, and it looks natural next to a tree or wood tones.
  • Classic red and green. Festive and cheerful; a plaid gives you both colors in one piece.
  • Neutrals with one red accent. Cream and oatmeal for most of the family, with a single red piece so the baby stands out.
  • Navy and gray. A quieter palette that suits winter outdoor photos.

Hold the pieces together in daylight before the photo day. Colors can look different under indoor lighting, so check that the shades read as a pair.

How to Size When Baby Is Growing Fast

Babies can change size quickly, so it helps to size for the day you will wear the outfit rather than the day you order. Compare the size chart on the product page to a piece that fits your baby well now, and read the chest and length measurements instead of relying on age alone. Age labels are only a guide, since babies of the same age can be quite different lengths.

If your baby is between sizes, sizing up gives room for a growth spurt and more comfortable movement, while staying true to size looks neater in photos. Measurements are approximate, so allow a little room for layers. Read the description for fabric and care details, and follow the care label once your pieces arrive.

For adults and older siblings, size each person separately from the chart. Our matching outfit sizing guide walks through how to size a whole household.

Easy Photo Ideas for Baby's First Christmas

  • Shoot after a nap and a feed, when a baby is most likely to be calm and awake.
  • Sit on a blanket near a window in the late morning for soft light.
  • Let parents and siblings lean in close, so the baby is the center of the frame.
  • Keep the background simple, such as a tree, a wreath, or a plain wall, so the outfits stand out.
  • Use a phone stand with a timer and burst mode, and take plenty of shots. Babies move quickly and the best photo is often a happy accident.
  • Steam or iron pieces first so prints and knits lie flat, and bring a spare outfit in case of spills.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do matching Christmas outfits come in baby sizes?

Some do. Many designs start at toddler sizes, while some include a 6-12 months size. Check the size list on each product page to see the smallest size for that design.

Should the baby match exactly, or just share colors?

Either works. Identical outfits look like a clear set in photos, while shared colors are easier if a design is not offered in a baby size. Choose the option that keeps the baby comfortable.

What size should I order for a baby who is growing?

Use the chest and length measurements on the size chart and compare them to something that fits well now. Size up for growing room, or stay true to size for a neater look in photos.

Can the whole family match with a baby?
