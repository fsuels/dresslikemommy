The easiest way to match as a family at Christmas is with shirts: pick one shared color or design, then choose each person's size separately. A tee suits a busy day of baking, shopping and gift wrapping, while a sweatshirt suits cooler evenings, parties and the family card photo. This guide explains how to choose between them, which palettes photograph well, how to size a group that runs from baby to grown-up, and how to make the day easy on everyone.

Tees or Sweatshirts: Which Fits Your Plans?

Start with the setting. If the plan is a warm house, a casual dinner or a day of activities, a lightweight top is comfortable for all-day wear, and you can layer a cardigan or jacket over it for the trip outside. Our family matching tops collection is the best place to compare tees, long-sleeve tops and sweatshirts in one view.

If you want something warmer, choose a crewneck sweatshirt or a knit. Sweatshirts are forgiving on kids, wash easily, and look relaxed in photos. When you want a more polished, dressed-up feel for a party, look at the Christmas sweaters or the family sweaters instead. And if the plan is a slow morning by the tree, matching family pajamas are the cozy choice.

Many families pick two looks: pajamas or a tee for the morning, then a sweatshirt or sweater for anything with guests. Whatever you pick, choose a design offered in every size you need, so the whole household can match.

Three Easy Ways to Match With Shirts

1. Identical shirts

The same design in every size is the classic family look. It reads at once as a group in photos and takes almost no planning beyond picking sizes. It is the fastest option if you are shopping close to a deadline.

2. Same design, different colors

Some designs come in more than one colorway. One example is a family sweatshirt that comes in cream and brown, with a small cartoon face on the chest and different faces for adults and kids. Wearing both colors together gives a coordinated look with some variety, and it works well when family members have different color preferences.

3. One shared theme

Everyone wears a different shirt, but the shirts share a color, a print style or a holiday motif. It is the most flexible option, and it suits families where people have strong opinions about what they will wear.

Color Palettes That Photograph Well

  • Cream and brown. Warm and neutral, and it looks natural next to a tree, a fireplace or wood tones.
  • Red and white. Classic and bright; a red top with a white one gives a clean, festive contrast.
  • Green and cream. A calm, natural palette that works both indoors and outdoors.
  • Black and white. Modern and easy to dress up with a bright accessory such as a red scarf or hat.

Hold the shirts together in daylight before the photo. Colors can look different under indoor lighting, so check that the shades read as a set. Avoid mixing very bright colors with very muted ones in the same picture.

Sizing a Whole Family, From Baby to Adult

Size every person on their own. Open the size chart on each product page, compare it to a shirt that already fits well, and choose from there. Measurements are approximate, so if a child is between sizes, sizing up leaves room to grow, while staying true to size gives a neater look for photos.

Age labels are only a guide, because children of the same age can vary a lot in height. Use the chest and length measurements on the chart rather than the age alone. Some designs include sizes for babies and toddlers as well as older kids and a wide adult range, so check the size list on each product page if you want to include a little one. Read the description for fabric details and follow the care label once your pieces arrive.

For adults, remember that a sweatshirt is usually worn with room to spare and a tee is often worn closer to the body, so check the chest measurement against how you like the garment to fit. If someone wants to layer a shirt over long sleeves, size up.

Photo Tips for Matching Shirts

  • Pick a simple background, such as a plain wall, a tree or a fireplace, so the shirts and faces stand out.
  • Shoot in soft window light in the late morning rather than under harsh overhead lights.
  • Steam or iron tees and sweatshirts first so the prints lie flat.
  • Give each person a job for the shot: hold the baby, lift a child, or lean on a shoulder. Movement looks more natural than stiff posing.
  • Take many frames with a phone timer or burst mode. Kids move quickly, and the best picture is often the silly one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should everyone wear the exact same shirt?

Not necessarily. Identical shirts give the clearest matching look, while a shared color or theme feels more relaxed. Choose the option the whole family will be comfortable wearing for several hours.

Are sweatshirts or tees better for a Christmas photo?

Sweatshirts feel cozier and add texture on camera, and tees are easier for active kids and warm homes. Pick based on where the photo will be taken and how warm the room is.

How do I size a child who is between sizes?

Use the chest and length measurements on the size chart. Size up for growing room and a relaxed fit, or stay true to size for a neater look in photos.

Can the baby match too?

Check the size list on each product page, because some designs include baby and toddler sizes and others start at older ages. Choose a design that covers every size you need.
